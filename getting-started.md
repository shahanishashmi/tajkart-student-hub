You do not need to study every subject at once. Choose one result you want to reach, use a small sequence of material, practise what you learned, and decide what to do next. Tajkart is built primarily for students; self-learners and early-career learners are welcome too.

## Use this learning loop for any subject

1. **Choose one outcome.** Write what you want to explain, solve, or build by the end of a study session.
2. **Study a small section.** Read or watch one focused piece. The [active reading guide]({{ '/learning/books/' | relative_url }}) suggests how to turn a chapter into recall and practice.
3. **Recall before checking.** Close the source and write the main idea in your own words, or work through one example from memory.
4. **Practise and check.** Try a question, exercise, or small project task. Compare your result with the stated example or test.
5. **Choose the next step.** Record one question, gap, or skill to revisit. A paper notebook is enough, or use the optional [study planner]({{ '/learning/planner/' | relative_url }}) to keep path checkmarks in this browser. It does not create an account, sync between devices, or claim mastery.

## Choose a route

### I am new to programming

1. Start with the [programming foundations guide]({{ '/learning/guides/programming-foundations/' | relative_url }}) to understand inputs, steps, output, and tests.
2. Follow the [four-week programming foundations course]({{ '/learning/courses/' | relative_url }}). Its modules link to lessons on variables, loops, functions, collections, and Git.
3. Check your understanding with the [interactive prediction routine]({{ '/learning/interactive-tutorials/' | relative_url }}) and [code-reading quiz]({{ '/learning/interactive-tutorials/quiz/' | relative_url }}).
4. Solve a [beginner problem]({{ '/learning/problem-sets/array-total/' | relative_url }}), then plan a [reading tracker project]({{ '/learning/projects/reading-tracker/' | relative_url }}).
5. When the foundations feel steady, continue to the [intermediate Python course]({{ '/learning/courses/python-intermediate/' | relative_url }}). This course is a draft awaiting technical review.

### I am studying computer science or IT

1. Use the [subject map]({{ '/learning/subjects/' | relative_url }}) to choose a topic that matches your current class or project. A mapped topic does not always have a complete course.
2. Begin with the relevant original guide, such as [databases and SQL]({{ '/learning/guides/databases-and-sql/' | relative_url }}), [operating systems]({{ '/learning/guides/operating-systems/' | relative_url }}), or [computer networks]({{ '/learning/guides/computer-networks/' | relative_url }}).
3. For algorithms, use the [data structures course]({{ '/learning/courses/data-structures/' | relative_url }}) and practise with the [binary search problem]({{ '/learning/problem-sets/binary-search/' | relative_url }}).
4. Make the idea concrete with a related [project brief]({{ '/learning/projects/' | relative_url }}), and explain what you tested and what remains unfinished.

### I am strengthening academic fundamentals or preparing for an assessment

1. Choose one topic or requirement from your actual syllabus, teacher, or assignment rubric; the 46-topic map is for computing, not a complete academic curriculum.
2. Use the original [study-planning and assessment guide]({{ '/learning/study-skills/' | relative_url }}) to set a checkable outcome, practise before checking, and plan a next step.
3. If fractions, decimals, ratios, percentages, or basic data reading are part of your course, try the beginner [mathematics foundations path]({{ '/learning/courses/academic-foundations/' | relative_url }}). It is a short original English sequence, not a whole math curriculum.
4. For reading-heavy work, use the [active reading guide]({{ '/learning/books/' | relative_url }}) to make questions and recall notes from a chapter.
5. For a practical assessment, choose a matching [project brief]({{ '/learning/projects/' | relative_url }}) and compare your work with its requirements and rubric. The brief is a specification, not a completed application.

### I am building skills for a job-ready portfolio

1. Choose one beginner route from the [career learning roadmaps]({{ '/careers/entry-level-roadmaps/' | relative_url }}) that fits your interests and current skills.
2. Practise a small part of that skill and create evidence with a suitable [student portfolio project]({{ '/learning/projects/student-portfolio/' | relative_url }}).
3. Use the [resume and portfolio guide]({{ '/careers/resume-portfolio/' | relative_url }}) to describe your contribution and unfinished work honestly.

### I am preparing for internships or an entry-level role

1. Compare the [entry-level learning paths]({{ '/careers/entry-level-roadmaps/' | relative_url }}) and select one skill to strengthen.
2. Build evidence in a small [student portfolio project]({{ '/learning/projects/student-portfolio/' | relative_url }}).
3. Use the [resume and portfolio guide]({{ '/careers/resume-portfolio/' | relative_url }}) to describe your work accurately, then practise with the [interview guide]({{ '/careers/interview-prep/' | relative_url }}).
4. Before applying, read the [internship preparation guide]({{ '/careers/internship-preparation/' | relative_url }}) and [student opportunity checklist]({{ '/offers/' | relative_url }}). Tajkart currently lists no verified live jobs, internships, scholarships, or offers.
