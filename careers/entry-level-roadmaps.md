A career roadmap is a study plan to explore a type of work. It is not a promise that a course or project will lead to a job. Requirements differ by employer, location, and role, so read current official descriptions when planning an application.

## Compare a few starting points

| Direction | Work to explore | Small evidence project |
|---|---|---|
| IT support | Diagnose a reported issue, explain steps, document a safe fix, and know when to escalate. | Write a troubleshooting guide for a fictional device issue using no real account details. |
| Software development | Turn a requirement into code, tests, and a clear explanation of trade-offs. | Build and test a small reading tracker or flashcard application. |
| Data work | Ask a question, inspect a dataset, query or summarize it, and describe limits. | Use a made-up study-session dataset to calculate totals and explain one pattern. |

These are broad examples, not complete job descriptions or statements about current demand.

## Build a 6-week exploration plan

1. **Week 1:** Read several current role descriptions from official employer pages; list repeated tasks and unfamiliar terms.
2. **Weeks 2–3:** Practise one foundation skill relevant to the direction and write down what is confusing.
3. **Weeks 4–5:** Finish one small project, test it, and document the choices and limitations.
4. **Week 6:** Ask a teacher, peer, or mentor for specific feedback. Revise the plan based on the feedback and the work you enjoyed.

Keep the schedule flexible and treat time estimates as personal planning aids. You can change directions after learning what the work involves.
