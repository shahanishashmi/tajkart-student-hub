## Learning objectives

Practise clarifying a problem, explaining a solution, checking edge cases, and describing your own work honestly.

## Prerequisites

Bring one project or programming problem you can discuss. Review the [subject map]({{ '/learning/subjects/' | relative_url }}) if an unfamiliar term appears in an interview description.

## Use a repeatable problem-solving approach

1. Restate the task in your own words and ask about unclear requirements.
2. Identify the input, expected output, constraints, and one small example.
3. Describe a straightforward approach before trying to optimise it.
4. Work through the example aloud, then implement or outline the method.
5. Check normal, empty, boundary, and unusual inputs.
6. Explain the cost of the approach when relevant; the [algorithm efficiency lesson]({{ '/learning/lessons/algorithm-efficiency/' | relative_url }}) reviews growth estimates.

If you get stuck, say what you know, make a small test case, and ask a focused question. Interview exercises assess communication and reasoning as well as a final answer.

## Prepare examples from your experience

Choose two or three true examples: a project you completed, a bug you investigated, and a time you changed your approach after feedback. For each, explain the context, your specific contribution, the action you took, and what happened. Mention a limitation or next improvement; reflection is more useful than pretending everything went perfectly.

Practise answering questions such as:

- What does this project help someone do?
- What part did you build, and how did you check it worked?
- What trade-off did you consider?
- What was a mistake you found, and how did you debug it?
- What would you improve with another week?

Use the [resume and portfolio guide]({{ '/careers/resume-portfolio/' | relative_url }}) to keep project descriptions accurate and easy to verify.

## A one-week practice plan

| Day | Practice |
|---|---|
| 1 | Read the role description and identify three skills it asks for. |
| 2 | Pick one project and explain it in two minutes without reading notes. |
| 3 | Solve one small problem and narrate your reasoning. |
| 4 | Test boundary cases and explain how you found them. |
| 5 | Practise one project question and one teamwork or learning question with a peer. |
| 6 | Review unclear answers; practise asking for clarification. |
| 7 | Rest, check the interview logistics, and prepare a few questions about the role. |

Adapt the schedule to your circumstances. Do not share passwords, private source code, or identity documents in an interview practice forum. Tajkart does not guarantee interviews, hiring, or placement.
