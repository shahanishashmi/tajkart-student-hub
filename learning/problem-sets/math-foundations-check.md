## Problem

Try each question without opening the hints or reference solutions. Show your steps. For real coursework, use your instructor's methods and rounding rules. This is a formative self-check; it is not an official test or a substitute for a teacher's assessment.

## Example questions

1. Simplify `18/30`, then calculate `18/30 + 1/5` as an exact fraction.
2. Convert `7/16` to a decimal and percentage. State whether its decimal terminates.
3. A 2.4-kilogram bag contains 0.6 kilograms of rice. What percentage of the bag is rice?
4. Four notebooks cost 120 currency units. Find the cost per notebook and state the units.
5. A 250-unit item is discounted by 12%. Find the reduced price.
6. A measured amount falls from 80 to 60. Find its percent change relative to the original and state the direction.
7. Four recorded study sessions lasted `12, 15, 0, 9` minutes. Find the mean, median, and total. What would a blank entry mean differently from the recorded zero?

## Hints

1. Simplify first; write `1/5` with a denominator of 5.
2. Compute `7 ÷ 16`; multiply the decimal by 100 to get the percentage.
3. Divide the rice amount by the whole bag amount, then multiply by 100.
4. Divide total price by number of notebooks and retain currency-per-notebook units.
5. Find 12% of 250, then subtract that discount from the original price.
6. Use `(new − original) ÷ original × 100%`.
7. Add all four recorded values for the total. Sort them before finding the median, and divide the total by four for the mean.

## Reference solutions

1. `18/30 = 3/5`; `3/5 + 1/5 = 4/5`.
2. `7/16 = 0.4375 = 43.75%`; this decimal terminates because the reduced denominator has only factors 2.
3. `0.6 ÷ 2.4 × 100% = 25%`.
4. `120 ÷ 4 = 30` currency units per notebook.
5. Discount is `0.12 × 250 = 30`, so the reduced price is 220 units.
6. `(60 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100% = −25%`; the amount decreased by 25%.
7. Total: `36 minutes`; mean: `36 ÷ 4 = 9 minutes`; median: `(9 + 12) ÷ 2 = 10.5 minutes`. A recorded zero is an observation. A blank is missing information and should not be counted as zero without an explicit rule.
